# How to Be Cultured on a Shoestring Budget: 7 Easy & Affordable Ways

You can easily take advantage of smart, creative ways to learn how to be cultured on a shoestring budget. Many people hold on to the outdated belief that being cultured requires a lavish lifestyle or a massive bank account, but that is simply not true. You can enjoy a rich, cultured life full of art, history, global perspectives, and refined experiences without emptying your wallet. If you want to know how to live on a shoestring budget while still broadening your horizons, check out these seven simple strategies!

## 1. Museums

If you’re planning on going to a museum, be on the lookout for specific days or times when entry is completely free. When I was visiting Barcelona, I discovered that the [Picasso Museum](https://museupicassobcn.cat/en) offered free admission during select hours on Sunday afternoons. My friend and I scheduled our entire weekend itinerary around those free hours so we could take full advantage of the opportunity. Beyond free entry days, check for student rates, senior discounts, or reduced evening admission fees. Regularly visiting local museums and galleries is one of the easiest ways to expand your cultural knowledge on a budget.

## 2. Food

Whether you’re grabbing a quick lunch at the office or getting together with friends for dinner, you need to eat anyway, so why not turn mealtime into a culinary adventure? Exploring authentic international cuisine is a fantastic way to experience different cultures. My roommate and I frequently took advantage of lunch specials at nearby Thai and Ethiopian restaurants, and we were pleasantly surprised by both the delicious flavors and the affordable prices. Many Asian and Latin American eateries offer generous lunch specials that are inexpensive yet filling, often giving you leftovers for the next day. Similar to [tasting new wines](#7), trying diverse dishes expands your palate on a budget.

Here are a few quick tips for exploring global cuisine on a budget:

- Look for weekday lunch specials at local family-owned restaurants.
- Visit international grocery stores for unique ingredients and snacks.
- Host potluck dinners with friends featuring recipes from around the world.

## 3. Movies

Watch movies! One of the best ways to become more cultured is to familiarize yourself with world cinema and classic films like _Casablanca_ and _His Girl Friday_. If you subscribe to [Netflix](https://www.netflix.com), you can access an incredible library of foreign films, documentaries, and classic movies for a reasonable monthly fee. Alternatively, you can visit your local public library to borrow DVDs and stream movies for free through services like Kanopy. Getting to know cinema classics also lets you pick up on pop-culture references in your favorite TV shows—shows like _Gilmore Girls_ are packed with classic movie references that make rewatching even more fun! Check out film details on [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com) to build your watchlist.

## 4. Read

Libraries exist for a reason, so take full advantage of them! Reading books by international authors, historical fiction, and non-fiction works opens your awareness to diverse topics, perspectives, and cultures. Beyond public libraries, you can trade books with friends or organize a community book swap. Joining a book club is another fantastic, low-cost way to discuss recent reads, gain new insights, and stay motivated. If you enjoy story structure, reading [classic movie screenplays](#3) or translated literature can also deepen your appreciation for global storytelling.

## 5. The News

Follow global news from a variety of international sources. To gain a well-rounded understanding of world events, search for articles published by news outlets across America, Britain, Australia, Canada, and non-Western international press. Reading international coverage gives you multiple perspectives on complex global issues rather than a single narrative. Staying informed about current events makes you far more cultured and open-minded in conversations. Try discussing recent international stories with friends to build meaningful connections.

| Cultural Activity | Budget Friendly Approach | Primary Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Museum Visits | Free admission days & student discounts | Deepens art & history knowledge |
| International Cinema | Local library rentals & streaming | Exposes you to diverse cultures |
| Global News Reading | Free online international outlets | Broadens world perspective |

## 6. The City

Whether you are traveling somewhere new or exploring your own hometown, take time to wander and engage with your surroundings. When touring a new city, stop to chat with local artisans, street musicians, and shopkeepers about their craft and community. Engaging in these conversations helps you learn authentic personal stories and local traditions. If you’re in your home city, explore a neighborhood you aren't familiar with. An afternoon walking tour can lead you to hidden murals, independent bookstores, local coffee shops, or even [visiting free museum exhibits](#1) you might otherwise have missed.

## 7. Free Wine Tastings

Keep an eye out for complimentary wine tastings at local wineries, boutique wine shops, or vineyard tours. When I visited [Château d'Estoublon](https://www.estoublon.com) in the south of France, I was pleasantly greeted with a complimentary wine tasting. It was an unforgettable experience and a wonderful introduction to wine appreciation! Many local wine shops and vineyards host free or low-cost tasting events on weekends, making it easy to learn about different wine regions and tasting notes without spending a fortune. Heading to a tasting with friends makes for a sophisticated and fun afternoon activity on a shoestring budget.

Living a lavish, cultured life on a shoestring budget is completely achievable when you know where to look. From free museum days and public libraries to international dining and local wine tastings, enriching your life doesn't have to cost a fortune. What are your favorite ways to stay cultured without breaking the bank?
