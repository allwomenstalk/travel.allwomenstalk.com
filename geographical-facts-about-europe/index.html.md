# A Beginner Friendly Way to Start Learning Europe's Topography

As a continent with a hugely diverse topography, the **geography facts about Europe** are going to be an interesting collection. Each country could easily make a contribution with a unique detail, as the landscape changes so dramatically across small spaces compared to other areas of the globe. Whether you are searching for **five facts about Europe's topography** to help with a school project, or simply want to explore fascinating **facts about the continent of Europe**, this list has you covered. Let’s take a short tour around some of the most notable **facts about Europe's topography** and discover what makes this landmass so unique! If you are interested in further travel inspiration, check out our guide on [gorgeous European cities to visit](https://travel.allwomenstalk.com/gorgeous-european-cities-to-visit) and start planning your next journey.

## 1. The Tallest Mountain in Europe

When it comes to **geography facts about Europe**, and in particular the mountains, most people will assume that the names at the top of the list will be the famous Mont Blanc or the Matterhorn. In fact, the tallest peak often cited is Khan Tengri in Kazakhstan, standing 22,999 feet high, which is some 4,500 feet higher than the next tallest, Mount Elbrus in Russia. While geographers debate whether these peaks lie strictly within the European border or in Asia, they remain towering giants of the region. If you love mountainous adventures, you can read more about these peaks on the [Peakbagger official site](https://www.peakbagger.com/).

## 2. The Largest Lake in Europe

Of the top 10 largest lakes in Europe, six are located in Russia, although one of them partly sits in Estonia. The absolute biggest is Lake Ladoga, which covers just under 7,000 square miles and sits close to St. Petersburg. Lake Ladoga is the world’s 14th largest lake by area, and Russia also claims the deepest lake in the world, Lake Baikal. To help you visualize the continent's massive bodies of water, here are the top three largest lakes in Europe by surface area:

- Lake Ladoga (Russia)
- Lake Onega (Russia)
- Lake Vänern (Sweden)

## 3. The Longest River in Europe

Europe’s largest and longest river is the Volga, running for 2,294 miles through central Russia. Surprising for such a massive river, it has a humble beginning, rising at just 740 feet above sea level in the Valday Hills to the northwest of Moscow. From there, it flows all the way to the Caspian Sea. The low starting elevation makes the Volga a slow-flowing river, creating a unique delta ecosystem. Learn more about the Volga and other water systems on the [Encyclopedia Britannica website](https://www.britannica.com/), and see how they compare to the continent's [largest lakes](#2).

## 4. The Highest Waterfall in Europe

The Krimml Waterfalls are the tallest in Europe and also fifth on the list of the world’s highest waterfalls. These spectacular falls are located in the Hohe Tauern National Park and are one of Austria’s most visited tourist attractions. Thanks to a well-maintained hiking trail, you can actually get very close to the rushing waters safely. If you are planning a trip to Austria, you can find visitor details on the [official Krimml Waterfalls website](https://www.wasserfaelle-krimml.at/en/).

## 5. The Longest Beach in Europe

The continent is awash with stunning sandy shores, so it’s only right that the **facts about Europe continent** geography include its longest beach. This is found at La Baule-Escoublac, on the southern coast of Brittany, France. The beach of La Baule measures 7.5 miles and is served by a lovely, historic seaside town where many of the French elite own holiday homes. If you love sun and sand, check out our guide on the [best beaches in Europe you have to visit](https://travel.allwomenstalk.com/best-beaches-in-europe-you-have-to-visit) for your next summer getaway.

## 6. The Deepest Cave in Europe

This honor goes to Gouffre Mirolda in France. It is 1,733 meters (5,686 feet) deep and is located near the Swiss and Italian borders, in the Savoy Alps close to the village of Samoëns. Gouffre Mirolda is one of the deepest caves in the world, ranking alongside Krubera Cave and Veryovkina Cave (both located in Georgia). Exploring such depths requires serious expertise, but the surrounding Alps offer plenty of surface-level adventure. For those interested in subterranean exploration, check out the [French Federation of Speleology](https://www.ffspelaeo.fr/) for safety guides.

## 7. The Tallest Volcano in Europe

Not only is Mount Etna the highest volcanic peak in Europe, but it is also one of the most active volcanoes in the world. Standing at 10,922 feet on the coast of the Mediterranean island of Sicily, it is an imposing sight. It is massive, measuring about 2.5 times the size of Mount Vesuvius, which so famously buried Pompeii in ancient times. Hikers and geology enthusiasts frequently visit this active peak to witness its steaming craters. You can find real-time activity updates on the [National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology website](https://www.ingv.it/en/). If you are looking for more Mediterranean travel ideas, explore our list of [beautiful islands in the Mediterranean](https://travel.allwomenstalk.com/beautiful-islands-in-the-mediterranean) to visit.

## 8. Biggest Glacier in Europe

As we have explored fire with active volcanoes, it wouldn’t be right to leave ice out of our **european geography facts**. Glaciers are found in a surprisingly large number of European countries, including Iceland, Norway, Spain, France, Switzerland, Austria, and Germany. It can be challenging to define the biggest glacier in Europe because 'biggest' is open to interpretation of length, depth, and width, plus they change over time. However, the consensus is that the largest glacier in Europe is the Vatnajökull glacier in Iceland. This massive ice cap covers almost 9,000 square kilometers (3,500 square miles), making it one of the largest glaciers in the world. For travel guides and glacier tours, check out the [Official Iceland Tourism portal](https://www.visiticeland.com/), and see how it contrasts with the warm shores of the [longest beach in Europe](#5).
