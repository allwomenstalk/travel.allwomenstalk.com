# What People Misunderstand About Italian Castles

When you think of European castles, the medieval citadels of Britain, the glamorous chateaux of France, and the fairytale castles of Germany come to mind. However, there are also some truly darling Italian castles that deserve your attention. If you've ever wondered how to say castle in Italian, the answer is castello—a word that evokes images of medieval grandeur and romantic history. While they might not be as widely celebrated as their European cousins, these historic fortresses are breathtaking. Here is a list of some of the best Italian castles and famous Italian castles that you will want to call home.

| Castle | Region | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| La Rocca Albornoziana | Umbria | Built atop a Roman acropolis |
| Castello Brown | Liguria | Yeats Brown's former consulate home |
| Castello Sforzesco | Lombardy | Located in downtown Milan |

## 1. Spoleto - La Rocca Albornoziana

![Spoleto - La Rocca Albornoziana](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/wf/m8/vhrvpqtt_600x337.jpg)

Perched above the charming hill town of Spoleto, La Rocca Albornoziana is one of the Italian castles you’ll definitely want to visit. Located in the southern Umbria region, this castle for the ages was built in the 14th century atop the foundation of a Roman acropolis. With widespread views of the village of Spoleto all the way over the gorge to the Bridge of Towers, Rocca Albornoziana was perched at such a height for the local political governors to use the castle as their seat. Six towers, two courtyards, and plenty of fabulous frescoes await you here. If you want to check ticket prices or book guided tours, visit the official [Spoleto Cultura](https://www.spoletocultura.it) site. Please note that the castle is only open for tours, so if you want to play pretend princess, you must arrange a tour at the ticket office at the castle’s entrance.

## 2. Castell’Arquato - La Rocca Viscontea

![Castell’Arquato - La Rocca Viscontea](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/s5/hf/au1yojav_600x450.jpg)

Another stunning hill town, serving as the backdrop of several films, Castell’Arquato is tucked away in northern Italy, in the region of Emilia Romagna. Rocca Viscontea di Castell’Arquato is the castle fortress which dominates the scene. This small castle is not overpopulated by tourists, so you’ll have plenty of time and space to breathe it all in. The fortress museum provides a history of the castle and the medieval times, encompassed in four rooms, along with a video which tells the tale. If you've ever wondered what do you call the top of a castle or what is the top of the castle called, these defensive architectural features are typically referred to as battlements or turrets. After a brief stair-climb to the very top of the castle tower, the whole of the town spreads before you, tumbling down the hillside while the vast countryside gleams brightly all around.

## 3. Portovenere - Andria Doria Castle

![Portovenere - Andria Doria Castle](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/d3/al/r34wwyki_600x398.jpg)

Located alongside the lovely village of Portovenere, Andria Doria Castle was built by the Genoese and lies on the Italian Riviera. With its centuries-long history, dating back to the 1100s, the castle is presently home to a charming art museum, which is open to the public in the summer. Narrow cobblestoned streets trail through the village to the castle, where you’ll find spectacular views of the sea. It's one of the most stunning sights in the region, much like other [beautiful places on the Italian Riviera](https://travel.allwomenstalk.com/beautiful-places-on-the-italian-riviera-to-visit/) that attract visitors from all over. On the edge of the promontory, the gorgeous San Pietro Church is also worth a visit.

## 4. Portofino - Castello Brown

![Portofino - Castello Brown](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/h3/cz/y11decs3_600x399.jpg)

With some of the loveliest landscaping to rival any of the snootier Italian castles, Castello Brown is situated in Portofino, a small village on the Italian Riviera. The castle’s pleasant garden and lovely sea views will have you lamenting your non-royal lineage. If you want to plan your visit or see event options, you can check out the [Castello Brown official website](https://castellobrown.com/). Castello Brown’s namesake is Yeats Brown, the British consul to Genoa who used to reside here in 1870. You could say the Browns still live in Castello Brown, as their pictures remain mounted on the walls, and their home furnishings still decorate the rooms. There have been many famous and celebrity visitors to Portofino, and you’ll be able to chronicle the “who’s who” in the castle’s photo gallery. Open in the summer and in winter, the castle is accessible via a footpath through the botanic gardens.

## 5. Pontremoli - Castello Del Piagnaro

![Pontremoli - Castello Del Piagnaro](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/r7/rk/hagf6upm_600x342.jpg)

Hovering over the preserved medieval town of Pontremoli, Castello del Piagnaro is located in the region of Lunigiana in northern Tuscany. This restored castle takes its name from the piagne (or slate slabs) which cover the area. Much like the defensive layout at [Castell’Arquato](#2), a fantastic view of the hilly landscape and of Pontremoli itself can be seen from the castle grounds. The Museum of Statue-Menhirs can also be found on the grounds, home to significant prehistoric steel and sandstone sculptures. The castle and museum are opened year-round, and only close to the public on Mondays in the winter.

## 6. Ferrara - Castello Estense Di Ferrara

![Ferrara - Castello Estense Di Ferrara](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/vk/0e/cazu32jm_600x450.jpg)

The Renaissance city of Ferrara is walled in and lies on Emilia Romagna’s Po Delta. With its Romanesque and Gothic architecture, the city is picture perfect, and the medieval Castello Estense di Ferrara only adds to the picture. You can view historical documents and tickets on the official [Castello Estense](https://www.castelloestense.it/en) website. Located in the city’s old town, you can view this majestic castle best from the front rooms at Albergo Annunziata. When touring this fortress, you will notice several distinct features:

- The water-filled moat that completely surrounds the castle walls.
- The underground prison chambers with their long, dark history.
- The hanging Orange Garden with its beautiful orange trees.

## 7. Milan - Castello Sforzesco

![Milan - Castello Sforzesco](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/xi/xg/htj4wyk0_600x450.jpg)

Want to dip into the most picturesque of Italian castles without traveling to an obscure countryside village? Milan’s Castello Sforzesco is not buried in the countryside, but lies right in the city’s downtown. In fact, you can walk to it from the Duomo. This urban fortress provides a stark contrast to the hill-topping setting of [Spoleto](#1). Built in the 15th century, Castello Sforzesco has undergone several renovations and stands strong for modern-day viewing. To plan your visit and check out the museum exhibits, check the [Castello Sforzesco official website](https://www.milanocastello.it/). Several museums have been housed in the complex since the 19th century, up until the present day.

They may not be world-renowned, but these gems of Italian castles certainly hold their own as top travel attractions.
