# Delta Flights Delayed Before Thanksgiving: What to Do

Delta flights were delayed across the U.S. on Sunday, November 22, when a system the airline uses to calculate aircraft weight and balance failed. Delta said it restored the system Sunday afternoon. Delays and cancellations were still showing Monday morning, particularly in Atlanta, so check your specific flight in the Fly Delta app or on [Delta’s flight-status page](https://www.delta.com/flight-status/search) before leaving for the airport.

If your departure or connection has changed, go straight to the [rebooking steps](#rebook). If you are deciding whether to travel at all, check the [refund rules](#refund) before accepting a replacement flight or credit. The reason for the delay matters when you ask for a meal or hotel, and the length of a significant delay matters when you decide whether to request your money back.

_Last updated November 23, 2026, at 12:21 PM ET._

## Why were Delta flights delayed?

Delta’s automated system for calculating an aircraft’s weight and balance ahead of departure went down on Sunday. The airline used slower manual checks while it worked to restore the system. At Delta’s request, the Federal Aviation Administration briefly held affected Atlanta-bound flights on the ground and then moved to a program that spaced out departures. The FAA told [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/equipment-outage-atlanta-causes-1300-delta-flight-delays/story?id=138360900) the disruption did not originate with an FAA air traffic system. Delta also said it was not a hack.

Delta said the automated system was working again Sunday afternoon. The airline told [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/delta-air-lines-nationwide-delays-amid-baggage-network-system-issue/) that about 70% of the delays in the FlightAware snapshot were connected to the automation issue, with weather and air traffic restrictions in the Northeast also affecting flights. An itinerary can have a different cause from the one in the headline. Ask a Delta agent which disruption applies to yours if you are seeking assistance.

## Did Delta issue a travel waiver?

A check of [Delta’s public advisories](https://www.delta.com/us/en/advisories/current-advisories) found no waiver specific to the November 22 weight-and-balance outage. Check your trip for any options Delta has offered on your booking.

The [Delta air traffic control outage waiver](https://www.delta.com/us/en/advisories/other-alerts/air-traffic-control-outage) is for the separate November 13–16 disruption. It says qualifying tickets issued by November 15 can be changed for new travel by November 23. If your booking falls into that earlier window, review the advisory and your trip today. Sunday’s Atlanta disruption does not automatically make a later Thanksgiving ticket eligible for that waiver.

## How to rebook a delayed Delta flight

- **Open Fly Delta or My Trips.** Check the status of every leg, especially a connecting flight. Turn on notifications and confirm Delta has your current email and phone number.

- **Look for an automatic rebooking.** Delta says it tries to put disrupted customers on another flight. Check the arrival airport, date and connection before relying on the new itinerary.

- **Compare alternatives.** Use Find Alternative Flights in [Fly Delta or My Trips](https://www.delta.com/us/en/change-cancel/delayed-or-canceled-flight). An airport kiosk or Delta agent can help if the app shows no workable options. Save the new confirmation.

- **Check together if you’re traveling as a group.** Pick one person to watch the app and share updates. Verify everyone has a seat before counting on the same replacement flight. Tell whoever is picking you up before they leave home.

- **Track your checked bags.** Look for updates in the app after rebooking. If a bag does not arrive, report it at the baggage service office and keep the reference number.

## When can you get a refund?

Under the [U.S. Department of Transportation’s refund rules](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), if an airline cancels or significantly changes your covered flight and you decline the alternative and choose not to travel, you are entitled to a refund. A significant change includes a scheduled arrival three hours or more later on a domestic itinerary or six hours or more later on an international itinerary. Other qualifying changes can include an airport change or an added connection.

The refund generally goes back to your original payment method, including the fare for the unused travel and applicable taxes and fees. A voucher or eCredit is an option when offered, and you can choose a refund if you qualify. If Delta cannot rebook you and you take no action within 24 hours, the airline says it will refund your original payment method automatically. If you take the delayed flight or accept and fly the replacement, DOT’s rule generally does not entitle you to a ticket refund for that trip. For a third-party purchase, check who sold the ticket and contact that seller about the refund.

## When can you ask Delta for a meal or hotel?

Delta’s [Customer Service Plan](https://www.delta.com/us/en/legal/customer-commitment) provides for a meal or voucher when a disruption within Delta’s control leaves you waiting at least three hours past scheduled departure. For a qualifying overnight disruption away from home or your destination, ask about a hotel and transport to and from it, subject to availability. Confirm the options with an agent before booking a room if you can. Keep itemized receipts if you pay out of pocket and use Delta’s [delay and cancellation guide](https://www.delta.com/us/en/change-cancel/delayed-or-canceled-flight) to find the reimbursement request.

Weather and air traffic control delays generally fall outside Delta’s reimbursement policy. Ask what the airline recorded as the reason for your specific disruption. A ticket refund and reimbursement for a hotel are separate questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why were Delta flights delayed on Sunday?

Delta’s system for automating predeparture weight and balance calculations failed. The disruption hit Atlanta especially hard and affected Delta flights elsewhere.

### Is the Delta outage still happening on Monday?

Delta said the system returned Sunday afternoon. Monday’s published counts still showed delays and cancellations in Atlanta. Check the status of your own flight before leaving.

### How many Delta flights were affected?

ABC News reported more than 1,600 Sunday delays from Delta, and The Points Guy’s later FlightAware recap counted more than 2,100 mainline delays. Those figures have different reporting windows.

### Is there a waiver for this Delta outage?

No outage-specific waiver was found in Delta’s public advisories at publication. Delta’s November 13–16 air traffic control waiver covers an earlier event.

### Can I get a refund or a hotel?

DOT provides for a refund on a canceled or significantly changed covered flight if you decline a replacement and choose not to travel. Delta’s meal and hotel assistance depends on whether the disruption was within the airline’s control and how long you wait.

### How do I change my flight in Fly Delta?

Open your reservation and check any replacement Delta has assigned. Use Find Alternative Flights if the itinerary does not work, or ask a Delta agent for help.

### Was this last week’s FAA ground stop?

Sunday’s disruption started with a Delta system. The earlier nationwide ground stop began with an FAA air traffic control outage.
